~/fit why 37signals, by way of Omarchy

I didn't arrive at 37signals by reading the founder. I arrived by running the things: a machine I can alter, plain files I can read, software small enough to finish. Omarchy is the first desktop that treated that as the point rather than an inconvenience, and it sent me back to the company that incubated it.

The Omarchy Doctrine runs ten lines. Five of them already describe how I work, which is the only reason this page is worth your time:

  • Own the machineMy own VPS, administered by me: thirteen services, no platform-as-a-service between me and the box. Omarchy on the desk for the same reason.
  • Command is serviceTwo secondments leading shop-floor experience. The job was clearing what was in the team's way and handing over the reasoning instead of the answer.
  • Welcome the agentsAgents in my daily loop, with explicit permissions and verification — and with the authorship of every repository stated plainly.
  • Beauty is truthRelease gates that refuse to publish on red. A README that says what a thing is not. Taste, spent on the boring parts.
  • You're somebody nowI'm a career changer with no computer-science degree. Your jobs page says you don't care where I went to school — which is fortunate, because the answer is: I'm still going.

The rest of the fit is the company, not the OS. Six-week cycles and a calm, low-distraction place to work are how I already operate: one thing at a time, finished, documented, deployed. A "Manager of One" is what running your own production estate makes of you whether you wanted it or not.

~/gap where I'm still learning

The gap is specific. I'll name it once and not decorate it.

  • Ruby and Rails — not a language I know. I'm not going to claim it to the person who wrote the framework. If it matters, tell me and it becomes the next thing I learn properly rather than the next thing I skim.
  • Backend engineering at depth — APIs and auth, observability, networking, the infrastructure around production systems. I run Docker, Traefik and nginx in production; I have not shipped enterprise software for five years, and I won't write like I have.
  • Omarchy contributions — I run it. I have not landed a patch. That is a thing I intend, not a thing I'm claiming, and it stays off this page until it's true.
  • Seniority — I'm early and I know it. I'd rather show you what I've built and let you judge the level than tell you a number.

What I do have: a decade of hands-on support, the habit of finding the fault instead of performing confidence, and the willingness to learn in the place where the system has to work. I learn this with an LLM and an agent harness beside me, and I'm after the 20% that makes a system operable — enough to deploy it, read what the harness proposes, and catch it when it's wrong.
